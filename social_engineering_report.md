# Research Report on Social Engineering Attacks

## Objective

The objective of this report is to study common social engineering attacks such as phishing, pretexting, and baiting. This report explains how these attacks work, their impact on organizations, real-world examples, and preventive measures.

---

# 1. Introduction to Social Engineering

Social engineering is a type of cyberattack that manipulates people into revealing confidential information or performing actions that compromise security. Instead of targeting technical vulnerabilities, attackers exploit human psychology.

Common goals include:
- Stealing passwords
- Gaining unauthorized access
- Financial fraud
- Data theft

---

# 2. Phishing Attack

## Definition

Phishing is a cyberattack where attackers impersonate trusted organizations through emails, messages, or websites to steal sensitive information.

## How It Works

Attackers send fake emails or messages containing malicious links or attachments. Victims are tricked into entering passwords, bank details, or downloading malware.

## Impact

- Identity theft
- Financial loss
- Account compromise
- Malware infection

## Real-World Example

Many fake banking emails ask users to verify their account credentials on fraudulent websites.

## Prevention Methods

- Verify email sender addresses
- Avoid clicking suspicious links
- Enable multi-factor authentication
- Use spam filters

---

# 3. Pretexting Attack

## Definition

Pretexting is a social engineering technique where attackers create a fake scenario to obtain confidential information.

## How It Works

Attackers pretend to be trusted individuals such as bank officials, IT staff, or company employees to gain sensitive data.

## Impact

- Data leakage
- Unauthorized access
- Financial fraud

## Real-World Example

Attackers may impersonate technical support staff and request employee login credentials.

## Prevention Methods

- Verify identity before sharing information
- Follow security verification procedures
- Conduct employee awareness training

---

# 4. Baiting Attack

## Definition

Baiting is a social engineering attack where attackers lure victims using fake promises or infected devices.

## How It Works

Attackers leave infected USB drives or fake download links hoping victims will use them.

## Impact

- Malware infection
- Data theft
- System compromise

## Real-World Example

Employees plugging unknown USB devices into office computers can unknowingly install malware.

## Prevention Methods

- Avoid unknown USB devices
- Use antivirus software
- Disable autorun features
- Educate users about cyber risks

---

# 5. Impact of Social Engineering on Organizations

Social engineering attacks can cause:

- Financial losses
- Reputation damage
- Data breaches
- Business disruption
- Legal consequences

Organizations are often targeted because employees may unknowingly expose sensitive information.

---

# 6. General Prevention Measures

- Conduct cybersecurity awareness training
- Implement strong password policies
- Use multi-factor authentication
- Regularly update software
- Monitor suspicious activities
- Restrict unnecessary access permissions

---

# Conclusion

Social engineering attacks remain one of the most dangerous cybersecurity threats because they exploit human behavior rather than technical weaknesses. Organizations and individuals must stay aware of phishing, pretexting, and baiting attacks and follow proper security practices to reduce risks.
